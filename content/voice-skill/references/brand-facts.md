## Founder / company facts

- She spent two years working in pharmaceutical formulation before starting Skinstinct.
  (`newsletter_001`, `newsletter_011`)
- She is not a dermatologist and has no medical degree. (`newsletter_001`)
- In 2021, at the pharma company she worked for, she sat in a stability review meeting for a

## Serum / formulation facts

- Skinstinct's serum pH is documented at 5.5-5.8, on every batch. (`newsletter_003`)
- Their contract-manufacturer brief requires: pH documentation, stability testing at
  three-month intervals for the first year, and mid-batch CoA sampling. Stated as "not unusual"
  requirements but "not standard practice for most brands." (`linkedin_post_001`)
- The original serum base was reformulated over "several months" until CoA results matched
  application-consistency testing; the label percentage of the active ingredient did not change
  during this process. (`linkedin_post_001`)
- The original serum base was too film-forming for high-humidity conditions: it trapped sweat
  and changed skin feel within 20 minutes of application in humid climates.
  (`linkedin_post_003`)
- Return data, 12 months to June 2025: 23% of product returns cited texture/feel as the
  reason; of those, 71% were from customers in cities with average annual humidity above
  70% (named cities: Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata). After reformulating to a lighter base
  with adjusted humectant-to-occlusive balance, returns from humid cities dropped to 8% in
  the following quarter. Active ingredient concentrations stayed identical throughout.
  (`linkedin_post_003`, referenced again in `newsletter_011` as "almost three times" the
  non-humid return rate before the fix)
- This return-rate/humidity data is described as "not proprietary" — they track and would
  share it because it's useful. (`linkedin_post_003`)

## Product line facts

- Skinstinct does not currently sell a Vitamin C product. As of `newsletter_004`, Meera had
  been trying to formulate one she was satisfied with for about 14 months. Blocking
  constraints: L-ascorbic acid above 10% concentration needs airless pump packaging, pH
  below 3.5, and a specific antioxidant support system to hold stability across 12 months;
  without all three simultaneously, concentration measurably drops within 4-6 months (this
  was found in Skinstinct's own formulation testing).
- Skinstinct does not currently sell a peptide product. No commercial stake disclaimer stated
  explicitly. (`newsletter_010`)
- Skinstinct's ceramide approach (for whichever product contains ceramides) focuses on
  ceramide NP and AP as primary types, paired with cholesterol and fatty acids to approximate
  the physiological lipid-matrix ratio, rather than ceramides in isolation. They do not currently
  market any product primarily on a "barrier repair" claim, and will do so "when we have the
  formulation data to support that claim specifically." (`newsletter_007`)
- Skinstinct's entire line is fragrance-free, including the preservative system, emollients, and
  any carrier ingredients where scented variants are common. (`newsletter_005`)
- Skinstinct does not use parabens — stated as "partly pragmatic and partly about customer
  trust," explicitly *not* because Meera believes parabens are dangerous at cosmetic
  concentrations (she states the evidence doesn't support that claim). (`newsletter_009`)
- Skinstinct publishes exactly what its product testing covers on its website: sample size,
  study type, who ran it. Described as "not the most flattering version" since some testing is
  described as early-stage. (`linkedin_post_004`)
- 12-month repeat purchase rate: 67%, against a stated market range of 20-40% for most DTC
  skincare brands in the same market. (`newsletter_011`)

## General skincare-science facts cited in the corpus (external, not Skinstinct-proprietary)

These appear in published pieces and may be cited again as established background — but
they are not Skinstinct's own data, so frame them as general findings, not brand claims.

- Niacinamide: water-soluble, relatively stable; does not oxidise like Vitamin C or degrade
  under light like retinol; converts to niacin at low pH (conversion accelerates below pH 4,
  starts earlier); niacin causes vasodilation (the mechanism behind flushing reactions).
  Sebum-regulation research starts around 2% concentration, more pronounced at 4-5%, some
  studies show benefit at 10%; below 2% is in cosmetic-preservative range rather than
  therapeutic. (`linkedin_post_001`, `newsletter_002`)
- Vitamin C: not a single ingredient. L-ascorbic acid has the strongest evidence for collagen
  synthesis support and antioxidant function but is the least stable (oxidises with air/light/heat,
  turns orange-yellow when degraded), effective pH range 2.5-3.5. Other derivatives (sodium
  ascorbyl phosphate, magnesium ascorbyl phosphate, ascorbyl glucoside, ethyl ascorbic acid)
  are more stable, formulate at higher pH, but have thinner direct efficacy evidence. Sodium
  ascorbyl phosphate converts to ascorbic acid on skin at a variable rate. (`newsletter_004`)
- AHAs (glycolic, lactic acid): function as exfoliants at pH 3-4, become basically inert above
  pH 4.5. Retinol: more pH-tolerant, still has a narrower optimal range; needs to reach the
  dermis for primary effect, so vehicle/emulsion type matters. (`newsletter_003`,
  `newsletter_002`)
- Fragrance/"parfum" on an INCI label can legally cover from a handful to several hundred
  undisclosed compounds. EU currently mandates disclosure of 26 specific fragrance
  allergens above certain concentrations (list has been expanded over time). Contact
  sensitisation develops over repeated exposure, is unpredictable in onset, and is permanent
  once established; estimated general-population significant-sensitisation rate is 1-3%, higher
  in people with existing skin conditions. (`newsletter_005`)
- SPF ratings are tested at a standardised 2mg of product per square centimetre of skin. A
  2022 University of Liverpool study measuring real-world application found most people apply
  0.5-1mg/cm² — roughly a quarter to half the tested amount; at half the tested amount, an
  SPF 30 product delivers protection closer to SPF 5-6. SPF-in-moisturiser is not a substitute
  for a standalone sunscreen for the same application-amount reasons, compounded by
  people applying even less moisturiser than dedicated sunscreen. (`newsletter_006`)
- Skin barrier (stratum corneum) lipid matrix composition: approximately 50% ceramides, 25%
  cholesterol, 15% free fatty acids, remainder other lipid types. Depletion/disruption raises
  transepidermal water loss and permeability to irritants/allergens/microorganisms.
  (`newsletter_007`)
- "Natural" has no regulatory definition in India and no standardised meaning in any major
  skincare market; not a verifiable material claim. Squalane exists in olive-derived and
  sugarcane-derived forms (both "plant-derived," different sustainability/purity profiles).
  Centella asiatica appears as whole-plant extract, standardised asiaticoside-percentage
  extracts, and synthetic madecassoside, under overlapping ingredient names.
  (`newsletter_008`)
- Paraben safety concern originated from studies showing oestrogenic activity at
  concentrations far above cosmetic-use levels; evidence of harm at actual cosmetic
  concentrations is described as "essentially absent." Some substitute preservative systems
  are less studied and cause more sensitisation than the parabens they replaced.
  (`newsletter_009`)
- Peptides: short amino acid chains; cell-signalling mechanism is real for specific peptides
  (named: palmitoyl pentapeptide-4/Matrixyl, acetyl hexapeptide-3/Argireline). In-vitro results
  are consistent; clinical results are more variable depending on study design, application
  frequency, concentration. Peptides are generally too large for passive stratum-corneum
  penetration without a delivery system. (`newsletter_010`)
